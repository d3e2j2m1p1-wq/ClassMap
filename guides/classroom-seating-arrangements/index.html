Classroom seating arrangements

There is no single best layout. Each arrangement makes some things easy and others hard, so pick the one that fits how your class spends most of its time.

Rows

Good for: tests, direct instruction, and classes that need structure. Every student faces the board and it's easy to see who's off task.
Harder for: group work and discussion.

Pairs

Good for: think-pair-share, partner reading, peer checking. Keeps the order of rows with a built-in partner.
Watch for: who sits with whom — pairs matter a lot in this layout.

U-shape (horseshoe)

Good for: discussion, seminars, and smaller classes. Everyone can see each other and you can walk the middle.
Harder for: large classes — it runs out of room fast.

Table groups (clusters, pods)

Good for: projects, stations, collaborative learning. Tables of four can also work as two pairs — see seating for group work.
Watch for: some students face away from the board.

Stadium (angled rows)

Good for: a middle ground — students see the board and each other, and it's easy to turn to a neighbour.

Mixing layouts

Many rooms combine them: a few table groups plus a row of single desks for students who work best alone. In ClassMap you can place tables of any size and single desks on the same chart.
