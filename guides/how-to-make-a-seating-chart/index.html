How to make a seating chart

A good seating chart takes most teachers an evening the first time. The trick is to decide what the chart is for, place the few students who need specific seats first, and only then fill in everyone else.

1. Draw the room as it really is

Start with the fixed things: where the board is, where your desk is, the door, and any tables that can't move. Then add the desks or tables the way students actually sit. A chart that doesn't match the room gets ignored after the first week.

2. Decide what this chart is for

Every chart balances a few goals. Pick the two or three that matter most this term:

  • Focus: separate students who distract each other.
  • Support: seat students who need help near you or near a strong peer.
  • Collaboration: mix skills, languages or boys and girls in each group.
  • Access: preferential seating from IEPs or 504 plans, eyesight, hearing, mobility.

3. Place the "must" seats first

Seat the students whose spot is decided by a plan or a need — front row, near the door, next to an aide. Everything else is easier once these are fixed.

4. Separate the pairs that don't work

Write down the pairs who shouldn't sit together and put them in different groups, ideally on opposite sides of the room. See how to separate students who shouldn't sit together.

5. Fill the rest, then mix

Fill the remaining seats so each table or pair has a mix. If you're balancing boys and girls, see the boy-girl seating chart guide.

6. Change it regularly

Many teachers change seats every 4–6 weeks so students work with new partners. Keep your old charts so you don't seat the same students together again.
