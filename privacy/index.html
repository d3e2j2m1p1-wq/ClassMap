Privacy policy

Effective November 25, 2026

In short: ClassMap has no accounts and no database. The class lists and seating charts you create stay on your device. If you choose to sign in with Google, a copy is kept in your own Google Drive. If you use Send & receive, what you send is locked on your device, passed through ClassMap's server to the other device, and never kept. We don't collect, see, sell or share your data.

What ClassMap stores, and where

When you use ClassMap, everything you enter — class names, student names, the groups and tags you assign, seating rules, notes, room layouts and classroom photos — is saved in your web browser on your device (in the browser's IndexedDB storage). It is not sent to us. Clearing your browser's site data for ClassMap deletes it, so we recommend using Back up everything to keep a copy.

ClassMap also keeps a few settings in your browser, such as light or dark theme, the first names it has learned for automatic sorting, and when you last made a backup.

Google sign-in and Google Drive (optional)

You can choose to sign in with Google so your classes are available on other devices. If you do:

  • ClassMap asks Google for your email address (to show which account is connected) and for permission to create and use files that ClassMap itself creates in your Google Drive (the drive.file permission). ClassMap cannot see, open or change any other file in your Drive.
  • ClassMap saves one file, “ClassMap data.json”, in your Google Drive. It contains the same information that is stored on your device.
  • Your browser talks to Google directly. The sign-in is kept only in your browser's memory for about an hour and is never stored or sent to us.

Import from Google Classroom (optional)

Teachers can import their class lists from Google Classroom instead of typing them. If you do:

  • ClassMap asks Google for read-only access to your Classroom classes and their rosters (classroom.courses.readonly and classroom.rosters.readonly). It reads only class names and students' names. It does not read email addresses, photos, coursework or grades, and it never changes anything in Classroom.
  • Your browser fetches the lists directly from Google and saves the names on your device, like a roster you typed. Nothing passes through ClassMap's server, and nothing is sent to us.
  • The sign-in is kept only in your browser's memory for about an hour and is never stored.

ClassMap's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from Google is used only to provide ClassMap's sync and roster import features to you. It is not used for advertising, not sold, not shared with anyone, and not used to train AI or machine-learning models.

Send & receive (optional)

Send & receive moves a class, a chart, or everything to another device — yours or another teacher's — without a file. Here is exactly what happens:

  • The sending device shows a short code (and a QR code). The other device enters it. Both connect, for up to 10 minutes, to a temporary meeting point on ClassMap's server (a Cloudflare Worker).
  • The two devices agree on a secret key between themselves. The server passes their handshake along but never has the key. Both screens show the same two check words, so you can confirm you're connected to the right device.
  • Nothing is sent until the sender taps Send. The class or chart is encrypted on the sending device (AES-256) and only the receiving device can decrypt it. The server only ever handles encrypted data, which it passes straight from one device to the other.
  • Nothing is stored: not the class, not the chart, not the code. The meeting point closes as soon as the transfer ends, either side leaves, or 10 minutes pass. Like any website, Cloudflare handles standard connection information (such as IP addresses) to deliver the connection.

Ask ClassMap (the helper)

The round button in the corner answers questions about the app and can make changes you ask for, like adding a seating rule. It is not AI: it is ordinary code that runs in your browser.

  • What you type into it is handled on your device and is never sent to ClassMap or anyone else.
  • It never changes a class or chart by itself: it shows what it will do, and nothing happens until you press Apply.
  • Earlier versions of ClassMap offered an optional AI model that ran in the browser. It has been removed, and if it was ever downloaded to your device, ClassMap deletes those files automatically.

What we don't do

  • No ClassMap accounts or databases. ClassMap's only server code passes Send & receive transfers along, encrypted, and keeps nothing.
  • No AI. Nothing you type is sent to an AI company or used to train anything.
  • No analytics, tracking cookies or advertising.
  • No selling or sharing of data, ever.

ClassMap's fonts and pictures are served from classmap.dev itself, so loading a page contacts no other site. Google is only contacted if you choose to sign in with Google or import from Google Classroom.

Student information

Teachers enter student names and seating notes to make charts. Because this information stays on the teacher's device (and, if they choose, the teacher's own Google Drive or another device they send it to), ClassMap does not receive or hold student records. Transfers with Send & receive are encrypted end to end and never stored, and the on-device AI processes everything on the teacher's device. Only send a class to another teacher when your school allows sharing that information with them. Please follow your school's policies about which student information you keep in apps — for example, using first names and last initials.

Deleting your data

  • On your device: delete classes and charts in ClassMap, or clear your browser's site data for ClassMap.
  • In Google Drive: delete the file “ClassMap data.json”.
  • Google access: sign out in ClassMap, or remove ClassMap at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
